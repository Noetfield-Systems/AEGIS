## Q1a — Phase 2 Scope (mandatory, pass/fail)
**Proposed response — revise against any official character limits:**

AEGIS is a modular AI Decision Assurance system designed to trace how changes in governance requirements affect registered AI systems, their controls, assessments, decision lineage, human reviews and evidence. Its core technical innovation hypothesis is an executable policy-impact compiler linked to native data/model/output measurement and deterministic historical reassessment, so users can compare an earlier evaluation with a recomputation on the same bound inputs and preserve a content-hashed chain of results.

The existing demonstrator implements versioned synthetic policy scenarios, affected/unaffected system selection, native risk/quality metrics, lifecycle work queues, explicit technical finding transitions, review journals and bilingual evidence exports. A government Directive HTML document was retrieved as original HTTP bytes and SHA-256-verified in GitHub CI on 19 November 2026. Six relevant Directive sections have been linked to proposed native controls; policy applicability and the mappings have **not** been approved by an independent professional.

**Essential outcomes:**
- **E1:** source references and versioned impact compiler trigger technical reassessment; independent complete policy corpus and representative systems remain open.

## Q4 — Science & Technology Risks (point-rated)
1. **Incorrect policy interpretation:** policy clause language is ambiguous or applies only to specified governmental decisions. Mitigation: archive official bytes; version/date and scope verification; expert-approved candidate mapping; unresolved state defaults to UNDETERMINED. Measure false-positive/negative mapping decisions.
2. **Evaluation generalizability and fairness:** fixed metrics may not capture semantic bias, drift or unsafe outputs. Mitigation: context-specific labelled benchmarks, fairness/robustness/semantic suites, blinded evaluation, calibrated thresholds and representative negatives.
3. **False reassurance from evidence hashing:** a hash proves content integrity, not source authenticity or legal approval. Mitigation: original-source provenance, reviewer identity, signed decisions, trust-chain verification and explicit confidence states.
4. **Interoperability schema drift:** upstream registry/AIA/telemetry formats change. Mitigation: versioned adapters, golden fixtures, schema-negatives, compatibility contract and source-specific validation receipts.
5. **Non-reproducible distributed AI operation:** model variance and data drift degrade repeatability. Mitigation: frozen input/model versions, deterministic controlled checks, manual adjudication and independent rerun protocol.
